Anatomy of an Ironless QDD Actuator

A low-cost quasi-direct-drive actuator built around a 3D-printed rotor carrying a Halbach magnet array — no iron back-iron — and a 7:1 planetary gearbox with cycloidal “lobe” teeth. Designed, built and documented by Caden Kraft; taken apart here from his original Fusion 360 model.

02 — Exploded assembly

A pancake, built inside out.

It is an outrunner: the stator’s teeth point outward and the printed rotor wraps around them. The gearbox does not sit beside the motor — it lives inside the stator’s 78 mm bore, with the ring gear keyed into the stator yoke at mid-plane. The article describes the gearbox module running “through the center of the actuator” with the stator assembled into its clam-shell.

03 — Inside the ironless motor

36 slots, 42 poles, no back-iron.

A conventional outrunner bonds its magnets to a steel ring that carries flux from pole to pole. Here the ring is printed plastic. Instead, 42 radially magnetised N52 blocks alternate with 42 thin N50 blocks magnetised sideways — a Halbach arrangement that pushes field toward the stator and cancels much of it on the outside.

04 — The 7:1 cycloidal planetary

Lobes, not teeth.

A standard planetary layout — sun in, ring fixed, carrier out — but every gear outline is a continuous wave of cycloidal lobes generated with the author’s modified version of Eelco Hoogendoorn’s pygeartrain.

Sun · input6lobes
Planet ×315lobes
Ring · fixed36lobes

ratio = 1 + Zring/Zsun = 1 + 36/6 = 7 : 1
check: Zring = Zsun + 2·Zplanet = 6 + 30 ✓ · (6+36)/3 planets = 14 ✓

Lobe counts were measured from the CAD geometry (the article gives only the ratio). The ring is modelled as two halves with opposite helix hands, i.e. a double-helical (herringbone) set; the article shows the script can export helical and double-helical variants.

How this visualization was made, and what it cannot tell you

Geometry. The shared .f3d file embeds Fusion’s display tessellation (≈1.06 M vertices, 287 unique bodies). It was decoded, decimated to about 175 k unique triangles with crease-preserving normals, and re-instanced for 750 placed parts. B-rep surfaces, tolerances and body names are not used.

Placement. Most occurrence transforms come straight from the file. The gearbox and controller sub-assemblies needed a parent transform that we reconstructed (a 90° axis change, plus a 24.4 mm offset for the board) and verified visually against the CAD preview. Small misplacements are possible.

Identification. Parts were named from shape, BOM quantities and the article — for example six identical 5×14×5 bearings, fifteen M4 heat-set inserts. The controller was rebuilt rather than taken from the CAD: outline and six Ø3.3 mm holes from Makerbase’s dimension drawing (63 × 58 mm), parts placed from Makerbase product photos (about ±1 mm), silkscreen redrawn, part heights estimated.

Motion. Gear and rotor motion follow ideal kinematics from lobe counts. Coil glow follows the published winding pattern at 21 electrical cycles per turn. Field sketches are illustrative. Nothing here is a structural, thermal or electromagnetic simulation, and no test was reproduced.
